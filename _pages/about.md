Research group under the Preclinical Addiction Research Consortium in the Department of Psychiatry at UC San Diego, studying neurodevelopmental and substance use disorders, with the help of rodent neuroimaging.



A fundamental challenge in neuroscience is understanding why individuals differ in their vulnerability to neuropsychiatric disorders and their response to treatment. Addressing this challenge is essential for developing more personalized approaches to prevention and therapy, yet the biological mechanisms underlying this variability remain poorly understood.



The lab seeks to uncover these mechanisms by investigating how individual differences in brain organization manifest across biological scales, from molecular and cellular architecture to brain anatomy, structural connectivity, and functional systems. To address these questions, we integrate complementary, scalable, and multimodal approaches, including whole-brain microscopy, longitudinal magnetic resonance imaging, systems genetics, behavioral neuroscience, and computational modeling, within the same experimental framework. Our goal is to determine how brain organization differs between vulnerable and resilient individuals before disease onset, how it changes during disease progression, and how it responds to therapeutic intervention. Our studies aim to identify features of brain organization that explain individual variability and establish quantitative biomarkers that provide a foundation for future precision medicine in neuropsychiatric disorders.
